1

SmartExam

Generative AIHackathon · Team of 4Working prototype

Two days, no sleep, one goal: build something that works and show real results before the deadline. Our team of four built an exam checker for Uniklinikum Würzburg. Drop in a Word exam, and a fine-tuned GPT-4o model checks every question against the exam rules, from allowed question types and forbidden trigger words to double negatives and answer-length cues.

We split the work, parsed exams into Markdown, checked every question in parallel and wrote the findings back into the original file as comments. By the final pitch it ran end to end on a real clinical chemistry exam template, ready to download.

2

Cislunar orbit families

Unsupervised learningSpaceCompleted · Aug 2026

Can an algorithm sort spacecraft orbits into families without being told their names? We built a dataset of periodic Earth–Moon orbits from NASA JPL, engineered features for state, geometry, dynamics and curvature, and compared 24 feature sets across K-Means, DBSCAN, Mean Shift and Affinity Propagation.

Using all four feature groups together worked best: K-Means found ten clusters for the ten known families, with the top one-to-one match score of 0.49.

3

Fast uncertainty propagation

Deep learningSpaceCompleted · Mar 2026

Monte Carlo is the reliable way to propagate a spacecraft's state uncertainty, but it is slow. We trained a neural network on thousands of simulated halo-orbit trajectories near the Earth–Moon L1 and L2 points to predict the propagated mean state directly.

It follows Monte Carlo closely on periodic orbits and loses accuracy on non-periodic ones, which shows where purely data-driven propagation stops being trustworthy.

4

Subseasonal weather forecasting

TransformersHPCCompleted · Sep 2025

Forecasting two to six weeks ahead falls between weather and climate prediction, where both struggle. We trained the Circular Transformer (CirT) on ERA5 reanalysis data and produced 2 m temperature forecasts for ECMWF's AI Weather Quest, next to FourCastNet and PanguWeather.

Training ran as SLURM jobs on the Julius 2 cluster in PyTorch Lightning, and every forecast was scored with the official Weather Quest tools.

It was less about leaving games behind, and more about expanding the kinds of problems I could solve.

Game development taught me how to build complex systems, think creatively and turn ideas into interactive experiences. Over time I became more and more interested in the intelligence behind those experiences: how machines can learn, reason and make decisions.

Machine learning felt like the natural next step, because it brings together software engineering, mathematics, experimentation and creativity. Instead of only building systems that follow predefined rules, I wanted to build systems that learn, adapt and solve problems we could never program by hand.

Next, I want to build intelligent systems that solve real problems and make technology more useful and accessible. Machine learning backed by strong software engineering, in products that go beyond impressive demos: systems that understand complex information, adapt to the people using them and make genuinely useful decisions.
